Accuracy and best practices

This page explains how to obtain reliable numbers and how to check them. The figures quoted come from the benchmarks of the accompanying paper (1D harmonic oscillator, BenDaniel–Duke quantum well, 2D oscillator); the corresponding checks are part of the test suite.

Checklist for a converged result

  1. Mesh convergence. Repeat the calculation with $h/2$. With exact quadrature the eigenvalues decrease and the change should shrink by $\approx 2^{2\ell}$ (16 for quadratic elements). The difference between the two runs estimates the error of the coarse one.
  2. Domain size. Enlarge $\Omega$. States that feel the walls shift upward. For bound states the shift decays exponentially with the distance to the wall. High-lying states and continuum states are always box states.
  3. Number of eigenpairs and shift. Check that sigma lies below the levels you want (or at the centre of your energy window) and that nev covers them all. Krylov eigensolvers can miss members of a degenerate level at the edge of the requested window. solve_tise and sweep_eigenvalues compute a few extra eigenpairs (guard_states) to prevent this. With the original input-file driver or eigen_values_and_eigen_vectors, ask for a few more eigenpairs than you need.
  4. Normalization and orthogonality. eigenstates_normalization and orthogonality_check should return 1 and ≈0.

Element degree and cost

At the same number of unknowns $N_h$ the cost is nearly independent of $\ell$, while the error drops dramatically with $\ell$. For the ground state of the 1D oscillator:

$\ell$$N_h$relative error
12559$3.8\times10^{-6}$
2319$6.3\times10^{-7}$
3479$2.5\times10^{-10}$

Use $\ell=2$ (the driver default) or $\ell=3$ through the low-level interface. Second-order finite differences behave like $\ell=1$, but approach the exact values from below.

Quadrature degree

The upper-bound property and the optimal rate assume exact integrals. With a polynomial potential of degree $s$, the integrals are exact for $k_q \ge 2\ell + s$.

Driver quadrature

The input-file driver uses $k_q = 3$ with quadratic elements. For $V=x^2/2$ this keeps the $h^4$ rate but roughly doubles the error, and the eigenvalues are no longer guaranteed to be upper bounds. When certified bounds are needed, use the low-level interface with $k_q = 2\ell+2$ or more.

Discontinuous potentials and masses: align the mesh

The weak form handles jumps of $V$ and $m^*$ exactly if the discontinuity lies on a mesh node (1D) or a cell face (2D). For a GaAs/AlGaAs quantum well with quadratic elements:

mesh$h$ (bohr)relative error of $\varepsilon_0$
aligned6.25$+9.3\times10^{-7}$
aligned1.56$+3.7\times10^{-9}$ ($h^4$)
not aligned1.56$-2.0\times10^{-3}$ ($h^1$, below the exact value)

With the uniform meshes of the driver, choose L and Δx such that every interface lies at an integer multiple of Δx from the edge of the domain.

Degeneracies on Cartesian meshes

Symmetries of the Hamiltonian that are not symmetries of the mesh are only recovered as $h\to 0$. For the 2D isotropic oscillator on a square Cartesian mesh, the $(n+1)$-fold degeneracies are split by $\sim 10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$ for $h=0.25$. States related by $x_1\leftrightarrow x_2$ remain exactly degenerate.

Performance tips

  • The eigensolver dominates the cost. Assembly is 10–20 times cheaper.
  • The solve time grows roughly linearly with $N_h$ in 1D and as $N_h^{1.4}$ in 2D. Typical times on a laptop: $0.1$ s for $N_h = 3\times10^4$ (1D) and $1.5$ s for $N_h = 6\times10^4$ (2D), $n_{\rm ev}=10$.
  • The cost grows faster than linearly with nev: $0.6$ s for 10 and $23$ s for 160 eigenpairs at $N_h\approx 2.5\times10^4$. Split large windows into several shifts.
  • In loops over a parameter, build the mesh, measures and spaces once.
  • Start Julia with threads (julia -t 4) to accelerate post-processing.
  • The first call of each function includes compilation time. Time the second call.
